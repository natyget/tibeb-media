On the desert road from Jerusalem to Gaza, the treasurer of "Candace, queen of the Ethiopians" sits in his chariot reading Isaiah out loud. A stranger, Philip, asks if he understands it, and the most powerful man on that road answers: "How can I, except some man should guide me?" We follow Acts 8 to the water, then trace who "Candace" really was (a title, Kandake, of the queens of Kush), what a Roman-era writer saw in one of them, where the baptism may have happened, the verse some Bibles footnote, and what the earliest Christian writers say happened next.

What is what in this episode:
- Scripture (every Bible): Acts 8:26-40; Isaiah 53:7-8; Jeremiah 38 (Ebed-melech); Psalm 68:31. Quotations from the King James Version.
- History: Kandake (Meroitic kdke) was the title of the queen or queen mother of Kush, capital Meroë, in today's Sudan (Pliny, Natural History 6.35); the "Ethiopia" of Acts is Kush, not the Aksumite highlands. Strabo (Geography 17.1.54) describes the Kandake of his day and her war with Rome (c. 25-21 BC). The Kandake of Acts was probably Amanitere (c. AD 25-41), a likely identification, not a certainty.
- History and tradition: at Ein Hanniya near Jerusalem, Israel Antiquities Authority excavations (2012-2016) found a Byzantine pool (4th-6th c.) at the foot of a church; some early Christians identified the spring with this baptism, and the Ethiopian Church still holds ceremonies there. A tradition, not proof.
- Text history: Acts 8:37 is not in the oldest Greek manuscripts; Erasmus printed it, and it reached the King James Version. The confession itself is the faith of the Church.
- Tradition: Irenaeus (c. AD 180) says he was sent into the regions of Ethiopia to preach; Eusebius (c. AD 325) calls him the first-fruits of believers throughout the world and says he "is said" to have preached first in his own country. Ethiopian tradition calls him Bachos; Acts gives no name.
- History: Christianity became the faith of Aksum's king, Ezana, in the 4th century, with Frumentius (Abba Salama), consecrated bishop around 330.
- Unknown: his name in Acts, the place of the baptism, and what happened to him afterwards.
